**How to read it**

- **At the bottom** = the last annual report public on the bottom date (a March-year report counts from 1 June). Prices are split/bonus-adjusted, matched to Screener's restated EPS.
- Market cap ≈ price × (net profit ÷ EPS); it can be off by the minority-interest share. ADANIGREEN, ADANIENT, TITAGARH, SOLARA use equity capital ÷ face value instead.
- ₹ figures are crore. Consolidated unless marked (S) = standalone. POONAWALLA, KIRLOSENG are treated as lenders (no debt or cash-flow ratios).
- **Winners only.** This shows what the 50 baggers looked like, not whether a trait beats other stocks. Many losers also look cheap and small. Section 7 has the one size test that does include losers (every NSE stock, point-in-time).
- Annual data misses quarterly turns, and Screener figures are restated rather than as first reported. Spot-check a few against the annual reports.

## 3. Where the gain came from

Market-cap gain = sales growth × change in price-to-sales. Across the 50: median sales growth bottom FY → peak FY **1.8x**, median P/S **0.66 → 7.9**. Only 5 of 50 grew sales 3x or more. **Most of every run was re-rating**: the market went from pricing the business as broken or ordinary to pricing it as a growth story, typically after one or two years of better numbers. The common thread is a low starting valuation plus a visible turn, not exceptional growth.

## 7. Size: price ≤ ₹200 vs market cap, tested on every stock

Our BAGGER SIGNAL fired during 26 of these 50 runs. Medians at the bottom:

| Group | n | Mcap | P/S | P/B | Debt/Eq | Promoter | FII+DII |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|

| Missed | 24 | 2,485 | 0.84 | 1.65 | 0.61 | 61% | 18% |

Among the winners, the missed names were bigger, so a market-cap rule looked like a better size filter. The lab tested that on **every** NSE stock-date, losers included (`bagger_lab.py`, market cap from NSE share counts):

| Rule (RS ≥ 90 + turnover ≥ 1.5x, plus…) | discovery 2015-19: names/day · 10x hit · lift | validation 2020-24: names/day · 10x hit · lift |
|---|---:|---:|
| price ≤ ₹200 | 9.0 · 1.51% · 9.84x | 16.0 · 1.98% · 4.69x |
| price ≤ ₹200 + mcap <= 1,000 cr | 3.4 · 1.61% · 10.53x | 3.6 · 6.21% · 14.76x |
| price ≤ ₹200 + mcap > 1,000 cr | 6.2 · 1.46% · 9.53x | 12.9 · 0.95% · 2.27x |
| mcap <= 1,000 cr (no price rule) | 5.2 · 1.02% · 6.66x | 5.6 · 3.87% · 9.2x |
| mcap <= 2,000 cr (no price rule) | 8.8 · 0.79% · 5.18x | 14.6 · 1.68% · 3.98x |
| price > 200 & mcap <= 2,000 cr | 4.7 · 0.55% · 3.59x | 7.7 · 0.23% · 0.54x |

- **Market cap does not replace the price rule.** At a similar number of names, market cap ≤ ₹2,000 cr did worse than price ≤ ₹200 in both halves. Small companies with expensive shares (price > ₹200, cap ≤ ₹2,000 cr) were a poor hunting ground. MAZDOCK and BSE were exceptions.
- **Market cap does sharpen the price rule.** Signal names under ₹1,000 cr hit 10x about 3x as often in 2020-24 and slightly more often in 2015-19. Above ₹1,000 cr the 2020-24 edge mostly disappears. This is the **S (small cap)** tag on the Next-Bull screen.
- Caveat: NSE share counts start in 2024; earlier market caps use them carried back, so companies that later issued many shares look too big before 2024.

## 9. Which fundamentals to combine with the technical signal

The technical signal answers **when** (RS, turnover acceleration, bull clock). Fundamentals
answer **whether** it can become a 10x. The counts below are from the 50 winners.

**A. Vetoes: skip the name.** These remove frauds and dilution traps; they don't find baggers.
- Promoter pledge above 25% of their holding, or rising quarter on quarter.
- Auditor resignation or qualified opinion, delayed results, insolvency, or an ASM/GSM stage.
- Operating cash flow negative in each of the last 3 years (non-lenders; 41 of
  46 baggers were cash-positive at the bottom).
- More than 20% new shares in a year without a named strategic investor.

**B. Size and valuation: room to re-rate.** Keep price ≤ ₹200; prefer market cap ≤ ₹1,000 cr
(the S tag, lab-tested).
- P/S under 1 (32/50) or P/B under 1.5 (31/50).

**C. The turn: confirm with the next quarterly result.**
- Coming out of a bad year (16/50): losses narrowing, sales recovering.
- Sales growth above 20% year on year (23/50 the year after the
  bottom), or operating margin up 3pp or more (35/50 by the peak).
- Borrowings falling (25/50).

**D. Ownership: who is still to buy.**
- Promoter at least 50% and not selling (25/43),
  or a new owner or strategic investor (POONAWALLA, ATGL, CEMPRO).
- FII+DII under 10% (22/45) and starting to rise.
- A fast-rising shareholder count is a late-stage sign, not a reason to buy.

**E. Narrative: written, not scored.** Order book or policy tailwind (defence, rail, power,
PLI), a commodity or pricing spike, new capacity, a demerger, a new owner.

**How to combine.** A is a veto. The score in section 6 ranks the remaining signal names.
To prove it adds lift, score the signal's **losers** the same way, starting with ~50
signal names that did not bag, and compare. Until then, use it as the research checklist
in the dossier.
